The Flexi Day iPhone app shows the same time off, groups and quotas you see on the web, signed in to
the same account. There is no Android app.

## What you can do in the app

- See the dashboard calendar, with your group's time off, public holidays, group events and
  birthdays.
- Request time off, attach files, and cancel your own requests.
- Approve or decline requests waiting for you, and comment on them, if you are an approver.
- Read the report for the groups you may see.
- Clock in and out and take breaks, if your organization uses attendance.
- Join a group with an invite link or code, and see your groups' members and quotas.
- Read the people directory, if your organization has it.
- Change your birthday, password and two-factor authentication, and delete your account.

## What stays on the web

Creating an account and resetting a password happen on the web; the app links there. So do creating
a group, group settings and permissions, the {{ui:nav.organization}} and {{ui:nav.billing}} pages,
calendar sync, and connecting Google, Microsoft or Apple to your account. The Assistant is web only
too.

## Signing in

Sign in with your email and password, or with Google, Microsoft or Apple. Two-factor authentication
works as it does on the web. The app stays signed in until you sign out or reset your password, and
signing out removes everything the app stored for you from the phone.

## Notifications and clock reminders

When you allow notifications, the iPhone gets a notice when your time off is approved, declined or
commented on, and when a request waits for your approval. These are the same notices as the bell on
the web, and the email setting in {{ui:nav.settings}} doesn't turn them off.

If your organization uses attendance, the app can also remind you to clock in at a time you pick on
the days you tick, skipping days off and holidays, and to clock out 15 minutes after today's required
time while you are still clocked in. The phone sets these reminders itself.

## Offline

The app keeps a copy of your time off, groups and quotas, so you can read them without a connection.
Changes, such as a new request or a clock-in, need a connection.
